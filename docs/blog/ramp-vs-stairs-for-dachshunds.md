# Ramp vs Stairs for Dachshunds

Ramps and stairs can help a [Dachshund](/dogs/dachshund) get on and off furniture. The better fit depends on your dog, the height of the furniture, the room layout, and the specific product’s design and stated specifications.

## Quick Comparison

| Feature | Ramp | Stairs |
| :---: | --- | --- |
| Walking surface | A continuous slope; check its length and incline against the furniture height and your dog’s comfort using it. | A series of steps; check the step dimensions and overall height against your dog and the furniture. |
| Footprint | May need more floor space to extend from the furniture. Check the product dimensions and the available room. | May fit closer to the furniture, but its footprint varies by design. Check the listed dimensions. |
| Moving or storing | Portability and folding features vary. Check the product’s weight, setup, and storage details. | Portability and storage needs vary by design. Check the product’s weight and dimensions. |
| Possible use | Choose one whose stated height range and intended use match the furniture or other access point. | Check that its stated height and intended use match the furniture where you plan to place it. |

## When a Ramp May Be a Better Fit

A ramp may suit a dog that is comfortable walking on a sloped surface, or a room where there is enough space to position one. The right length and incline depend on the height it needs to reach and the product’s specifications. Before choosing, see our [dog-ramp buying guide](/blog/how-to-choose-a-dog-ramp) for details to compare.

Some ramps are designed to fold or move between locations, while others are intended to stay in place. Check the product information before assuming a ramp will be easy to carry, store, or use in a vehicle. A ramp made for furniture may not be designed for car access.

## When Stairs May Be a Better Fit

Stairs may work well when a ramp would extend into a walkway or there is limited floor space beside the furniture. Their step height and depth vary, so check the dimensions and consider whether your dog is comfortable using that particular design. Some dogs may prefer steps; others may find a ramp easier to use.

## What to Check on Either Product

- **Grip:** Look for the surface material and any grip features listed by the manufacturer. A textured or rubber surface does not guarantee that a product will provide enough traction for every dog or floor.
- **Stability:** Check how the product is supported, whether it is intended to rest against or attach to furniture, and any setup instructions. Follow the manufacturer’s guidance for placement and use.
- **Dimensions and fit:** Compare the product’s stated height, length or step dimensions, and footprint with the furniture and available floor space.
- **Portability and storage:** Check the listed weight, folding or storage features, and how the product is moved or set up.
- **Vehicle use:** If you want help with car access, confirm that the product is specifically intended for that use and matches the vehicle’s access point. Do not assume a furniture ramp is suitable for a car.

The best choice is the product that fits your dog, the furniture, and the space—and that your dog is comfortable using. Compare the product’s stated specifications and follow its setup instructions.
